## Sub-section 1: Heading and long-term brain health (≈80 words)
**Job:** show the health problem is real and heading is the main suspect, but that these studies can't tell us *which impacts* matter.

| Move | What to write | Facts to use |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Describe (2 sentences) | Compare the two big cohort studies, Scotland and Sweden | Scotland: ≈3.5× risk of death from neurodegenerative disease [1]; risk higher with longer careers and highest in defenders [2]. Sweden: 6,007 elite players, ≈1.5× risk [11]. Both: goalkeepers not at significantly raised risk [2, 11] |
| 2. Evaluate (1 sentence) | Say what these studies **can't** show | They show association, not cause; mostly mid-20th-century players; how often and how hard players headed the ball was never measured |
| 3. Relate (1 sentence) | Bring in the laboratory evidence and link to sub-section 2 | One routine heading session caused short-lived changes in brain function and memory [4], so the size of each impact matters |

**Sentence starters:** "Large cohort studies have linked…" · "However, these studies…" · "Laboratory evidence suggests…"

**Tip:** don't repeat your introduction word for word. Comparing *two* countries (3.5× vs 1.5×) is synthesis, and that scores higher.

## Sub-section 2: How severe is a single header? (≈80 words)
**Job:** show what has been measured, that single headers are mild, and why measurement methods matter.

| Move | What to write | Facts to use |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Describe (1 sentence) | The classic measurement | ≈16 g at 9 m/s and ≈20 g at 12 m/s; HIC far below injury levels; **only 4 players** [3] |
| 2. Describe (1 sentence) | Newer sensors | Mouthguard sensors (iMGs) fit tightly to the teeth, so they move with the skull. Laboratory headers: 26 ± 7.9 g and 1730 ± 611 rad/s² [12]. In 133 match headers, goal kicks gave the highest angular acceleration [13] |
| 3. Evaluate (1 sentence) | Why values differ between studies | Ball speed, how the ball is delivered, sensor mounting and small samples |
| 4. Relate (1 sentence) | Your limitation | Concussion risk depends on linear **and** rotational acceleration [14]. Your rig measures linear acceleration first, with rotation as future work |

**Sentence starters:** "Measured head accelerations during heading are…" · "More recently, instrumented mouthguards…" · "Because concussion risk depends on…"

## Sub-section 3: Effect of ball properties (≈110 words). The key section
**Job:** set out the **conflict** in the evidence on inflation pressure, and suggest why the studies disagree.

| Move | What to write | Facts to use |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Context (1 sentence) | What the rules allow | Law 2: 0.6–1.1 atm; 410–450 g [15] |
| 2. Theory (1 sentence) | What a model predicted | Hertz contact model: pressure has **little effect** on the impact [16] |
| 3. Laboratory evidence (1–2 sentences) | Group the studies that **did** find an effect | Halving pressure cut head response by 10–31% (numerical model + 3 volunteers) [7]. Test head and neck at 34–76 kPa: lower pressure, lower linear and rotational acceleration [17]. Ball cannon at ≈17 m/s, 0.6 vs 1.0 bar: lower pressure helped in most cases, but is "not a universal solution" [18] |
| 4. Players (1 sentence) | Contrast with the human study | 12 players at 0.4 vs 0.54 atm: lower impact force but **no change** in head acceleration [19] |
| 5. Your explanation (1–2 sentences) | **Critical thinking:** why might they disagree? | (a) Players brace their necks, while the laboratory tests used a fixed neck. (b) [19] tested a narrow pressure range at low speeds (5 and 8 m/s). Which explanation is right hasn't been tested, which leads to sub-section 4 |

**Sentence starters:** "Law 2 permits…" · "A theoretical model predicted…" · "Laboratory studies, however, …" · "In contrast, when players…" · "One possible explanation is…"

**Tip:** use comparison words ("whereas", "in contrast", "similarly"). This is the clearest place to show synthesis, and markers look for it.

## Sub-section 4: Role of the neck (≈90 words)
**Job:** show that the neck seems to matter, but *how* it helps has never been measured directly.

| Move | What to write | Facts to use |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Describe (1–2 sentences) | What the studies of players found | Stronger and more balanced necks go with lower head acceleration [8–10]. In 100 players, head/neck size explained ≈22% and neck strength ≈13–17% of the variation [9] |
| 2. Describe (1 sentence) | What theory predicts | A rigid neck reduces head acceleration and HIC [16] |
| 3. Contrast (1 sentence) | The training studies disagree | 6 weeks of neck training: no clear benefit (33 players) [20]. Pilot trial with 52 adolescents: −11.8% vs −5.0% in controls [21] |
| 4. Evaluate + relate (1 sentence) | The mechanism is untested | Most of the evidence is correlations or small trials. The idea that a stiffer neck "couples" the head to the torso, raising its effective mass, is assumed but has not been measured. Neck stiffness can't be varied on its own in people, but **a rig can** |

**Sentence starters:** "Stronger necks have been associated with…" · "Training studies, however, …" · "The proposed mechanism is that…"

## Sub-section 5: Test methods and research gap (≈90 words)
**Job:** justify your method, then state the gap and your research questions. This is the most important paragraph in the section.

| Move | What to write | Facts to use |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Compare methods (1–2 sentences) | Strengths and weaknesses of each approach | Volunteers: realistic, but variable and need ethics approval. Anthropomorphic headforms: repeatable but costly (e.g. [17]). Mouthguards: real field data, but need validation [12, 13] |
| 2. Enabler (1 sentence) | Why your rig is now possible | Low-cost ±200 g MEMS accelerometers [22] make a cheap instrumented headform feasible |
| 3. **The gap** (1–2 sentences) | What is not known | This review found no *experimental* study that varied pressure, speed and neck stiffness together; only a theoretical model has combined ball properties with neck stiffness [16]. So it is unclear whether neck coupling explains the conflict in sub-section 3 |
| 4. Research questions (1 sentence) | Your RQs, in your own words | RQ1: how do speed and pressure change head acceleration on a validated low-cost headform? RQ2: how stiff must the neck coupling be to reduce it? RQ3: does stiffer coupling reduce the benefit of lower pressure? |

**Sentence starters:** "Three approaches have been used to…" · "Low-cost sensors now…" · "This review found no…" · "This project will therefore address…"

**Hedge the gap.** Write "this review found no experimental study…", not "no one has ever…". You can only speak for the papers you searched.

**Match check:** the gap and RQs must line up with the aim in your introduction (speed, pressure, neck coupling).

## Writing reminders
- **Each key study: describe → evaluate → relate.** A finding *and* a limitation *and* why it matters to you.
- **Group studies by theme and compare them.** Don't write one paragraph per paper.
- **Numbers with units.** Write "≈20 g at 12 m/s", not "a large acceleration".
- **Cite right after the claim:** "…lower acceleration [17]."
- **Use the past tense for findings** ("found", "reported", "measured").
- **Hedge sensibly:** "suggests", "may", "was associated with".
- **Your own words throughout.** No quotes, and no sentences copied from papers or drafts.
