## Football heading and head impact
[1] Mackay, D.F., Russell, E.R., Stewart, K., MacLean, J.A., Pell, J.P. and Stewart, W. (2019) "Neurodegenerative disease mortality among former professional soccer players", *New England Journal of Medicine*, 381(19), 1801–1808. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1908483
*Use for:* motivation. Ex-players about 3.5× more likely to die with neurodegenerative disease (1.7% vs 0.5%).

[2] Russell, E.R., Mackay, D.F., Stewart, K., MacLean, J.A., Pell, J.P. and Stewart, W. (2021) "Association of field position and career length with risk of neurodegenerative disease in male former professional soccer players", *JAMA Neurology*, 78(9), 1057–1063. doi:10.1001/jamaneurol.2021.2403
*Use for:* goalkeepers not at raised risk, defenders highest (HR ≈ 5); risk similar for players born 1910–1969 (useful for the vintage-ball idea).

[3] Di Virgilio, T.G., Hunter, A., Wilson, L., Stewart, W., Goodall, S., Howatson, G., Donaldson, D.I. and Ietswaart, M. (2016) "Evidence for acute electrophysiological and cognitive changes following routine soccer heading", *EBioMedicine*, 13, 66–71. doi:10.1016/j.ebiom.2016.10.029 ⚠️ *check the full author list*
*Use for:* a routine heading session (balls from a machine) caused short-term changes in brain function and memory, back to normal within 24 h.

[4] Naunheim, R.S., Bayly, P.V., Standeven, J., Neubauer, J.S., Lewis, L.M. and Genin, G.M. (2003) "Linear and angular head accelerations during heading of a soccer ball", *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*, 35(8), 1406–1412. doi:10.1249/01.MSS.0000078933.84527.AE
*Use for:* measured head accelerations, about 158 m/s² (16 g) at 9 m/s and 199 m/s² (20 g) at 12 m/s. HIC and GSI well below injury levels. This is the validation data for the model.

[5] Shewchenko, N., Withnall, C., Keown, M., Gittens, R. and Dvorak, J. (2005) "Heading in football. Part 3: Effect of ball properties on head response", *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 39(Suppl 1), i33–i39. doi:10.1136/bjsm.2005.019059
*Use for:* halving ball pressure reduced head/neck response by about 10–31%; ball construction up to 15%; ball mass matters. Parts 1 and 2 (same supplement) describe test methods and are worth reading.

[6] Dezman, Z.D.W., Ledet, E.H. and Kerr, H.A. (2013) "Neck strength imbalance correlates with increased head acceleration in soccer heading", *Sports Health*, 5(4), 320–326. doi:10.1177/1941738113480935
*Use for:* imbalance between neck flexor and extensor strength is linked to higher head acceleration.

[7] Caccese, J.B., Buckley, T.A., Tierney, R.T., Arbogast, K.B., Rose, W.C., Glutting, J.J. and Kaminski, T.W. (2018) "Head and neck size and neck strength predict linear and rotational acceleration during purposeful soccer heading", *Sports Biomechanics*, 17(4), 462–476. doi:10.1080/14763141.2017.1360385
*Use for:* 100 players; head/neck size explained about 22% and neck strength about 13–17% of the variation in head acceleration.

[8] Peek, K., Elliott, J.M. and Orr, R. (2020) "Higher neck strength is associated with lower head acceleration during purposeful heading in soccer: a systematic review", *Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport*, 23(5), 453–462. doi:10.1016/j.jsams.2019.11.004
*Use for:* review evidence that stronger necks go with lower head acceleration.

[9] Rowson, S. and Duma, S.M. (2013) "Brain injury prediction: assessing the combined probability of concussion using linear and rotational head acceleration", *Annals of Biomedical Engineering*, 41(5), 873–882. doi:10.1007/s10439-012-0731-0
*Use for:* why rotational as well as linear acceleration matters (injury metrics).

[10] The Football Association (2021) *Heading guidance for the 2021/22 season* (adult football). https://www.englandfootball.com/learn/Brain-Health/Heading-in-Football (accessed: add your date)
*Use for:* professional training guidance of max 10 "higher-force" headers per week.

[11] The Football Association (2024) *New heading rules for grassroots football youth matches*. https://www.englandfootball.com/articles/2024/May/17/New-heading-rules-for-grassroots-football-youth-matches (accessed: add your date)
*Use for:* deliberate heading phased out of U7–U11 matches (U11 from 2026/27), following the IFAB trial.

[12] IFAB (2026) *Laws of the Game 2026/27, Law 2: The Ball*. https://www.thefa.com/football-rules-governance/lawsandrules/laws/football-11-11/law-2---the-ball (accessed: add your date)
*Use for:* ball 68–70 cm, 410–450 g, pressure 0.6–1.1 atm (600–1,100 g/cm²) at sea level. Confirmed in the 2025/26 Law 2 text.

[13] Cross, R. (1999) "The bounce of a ball", *American Journal of Physics*, 67(3), 222–227. doi:10.1119/1.19229
*Use for:* ball impact physics and how to measure ball bounce and energy loss.

[14] Hunt, K.H. and Crossley, F.R.E. (1975) "Coefficient of restitution interpreted as damping in vibroimpact", *Journal of Applied Mechanics*, 42(2), 440–445. doi:10.1115/1.3423596
*Use for:* the damping model used in the code.

[15] Analog Devices (n.d.) *ADXL375: 3-axis, ±200 g digital MEMS accelerometer, data sheet*. https://www.analog.com/en/products/adxl375.html (accessed: add your date)
*Use for:* sensor choice (±200 g, up to 3200 Hz output rate).

## Running shoes and injury
[16] van Gent, R.N., Siem, D., van Middelkoop, M., van Os, A.G., Bierma-Zeinstra, S.M.A. and Koes, B.W. (2007) "Incidence and determinants of lower extremity running injuries in long distance runners: a systematic review", *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 41(8), 469–480. ⚠️ *from memory, check*
*Use for:* how common running injuries are (about 19–79% across studies).

[17] Nigg, B.M., Baltich, J., Hoerzer, S. and Enders, H. (2015) "Running shoes and running injuries: mythbusting and a proposal for two new paradigms: 'preferred movement path' and 'comfort filter'", *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 49(20), 1290–1294. ⚠️ *from memory, check*
*Use for:* the counter-argument that impact forces and pronation are weak injury predictors.

[18] Davis, I.S., Bowser, B.J. and Mullineaux, D.R. (2016) "Greater vertical impact loading in female runners with medically diagnosed injuries: a prospective investigation", *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 50(14), 887–892. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2015-094579
*Use for:* loading rate above 66 BW/s → odds ratio 2.72 (wide confidence interval) for medically diagnosed injury.

[19] Malisoux, L., Delattre, N., Urhausen, A. and Theisen, D. (2020) "Shoe cushioning influences the running injury risk according to body mass: a randomized controlled trial involving 848 recreational runners", *American Journal of Sports Medicine*, 48(2), 473–480. doi:10.1177/0363546519892578
*Use for:* hard (94.9 N/mm) vs soft (61.3 N/mm) shoes; higher risk in hard shoes for lighter runners. These stiffness values are used in the shoe model.

[20] Cook, S.D., Kester, M.A. and Brunet, M.E. (1985) "Shock absorption characteristics of running shoes", *American Journal of Sports Medicine*, 13(4), 248–253.
*Use for:* loss of shock absorption with mileage (large losses within a few hundred miles; read it for the exact figures).

[21] Verdejo, R. and Mills, N.J. (2004) "Heel–shoe interactions and the durability of EVA foam running-shoe midsoles", *Journal of Biomechanics*, 37(9), 1379–1386. doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2003.12.022
*Use for:* peak heel pressure +100% after 500 km; foam damage after 750 km; FE model of the heel and midsole.

[22] Kong, P.W., Candelaria, N.G. and Smith, D.R. (2009) "Running in new and worn shoes: a comparison of three types of cushioning footwear", *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 43(10), 745–749.
*Use for:* runners adapt their technique after 200 miles in worn shoes.

[23] Shorten, M. and Mientjes, M.I.V. (2011) "The 'heel impact' force peak during running is neither 'heel' nor 'impact' and does not quantify shoe cushioning effects", *Footwear Science*, 3(1), 41–58. doi:10.1080/19424280.2010.542186
*Use for:* measure the high-frequency impact part, not the "impact peak".

[24] Lieberman, D.E., Venkadesan, M., Werbel, W.A., Daoud, A.I., D'Andrea, S., Davis, I.S., Mang'Eni, R.O. and Pitsiladis, Y. (2010) "Foot strike patterns and collision forces in habitually barefoot versus shod runners", *Nature*, 463(7280), 531–535. doi:10.1038/nature08723
*Use for:* the "effective mass" idea used in the shoe model; foot-strike pattern.

[25] Hoogkamer, W., Kipp, S., Frank, J.H., Farina, E.M., Luo, G. and Kram, R. (2018) "A comparison of the energetic cost of running in marathon racing shoes", *Sports Medicine*, 48(4), 1009–1019. doi:10.1007/s40279-017-0811-2
*Use for:* "super-shoes" save about 4% energy.

[26] ASTM International (2024) *ASTM F1976-24 Standard Test Method for Impact Attenuation of Athletic Shoe Cushioning Systems and Materials*. West Conshohocken, PA: ASTM.
*Use for:* drop-test method (8.5 kg mass, about 5 J for running shoes). Get the standard from the library.

[27] Liu, W. and Nigg, B.M. (2000) "A mechanical model to determine the influence of masses and mass distribution on the impact force during running", *Journal of Biomechanics*, 33(2), 219–224.
*Use for:* mass–spring–damper running models (background for the shoe model).

## Added 13 November 2026: recent and extra sources (all checked online)
[32] Ueda, P., Pasternak, B., Lim, C.E., et al. (2023) "Neurodegenerative disease among male elite football (soccer) players in Sweden: a cohort study", *The Lancet Public Health*, 8(4), e256–e265. doi:10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00027-0
*Use for:* about 1.5× risk in 6,007 Swedish top-division players; goalkeepers not significantly raised.

[33] Barnes-Wood, M., McCloskey, H., Connelly, S., Gilchrist, M.D., Ni Annaidh, A. and Theobald, P.S. (2024) "Investigation of head kinematics and brain strain response during soccer heading using a custom-fit instrumented mouthguard", *Annals of Biomedical Engineering*, 52, 934–945. doi:10.1007/s10439-023-03430-8
*Use for:* laboratory headers measured with a mouthguard: 26 ± 7.9 g, 1730 ± 611 rad/s².

[34] Sokol-Randell, D., Stelzer-Hiller, O.W., Allan, D. and Tierney, G. (2023) "Heads up! A biomechanical pilot investigation of soccer heading using instrumented mouthguards (iMGs)", *Applied Sciences*, 13(4), 2639. doi:10.3390/app13042639
*Use for:* 133 match headers; goal kicks gave the highest angular acceleration.

[35] Queen, R.M., Weinhold, P.S., Kirkendall, D.T. and Yu, B. (2003) "Theoretical study of the effect of ball properties on impact force in soccer heading", *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*, 35(12), 2069–2076. doi:10.1249/01.MSS.0000099081.20125.A5
*Use for:* theory says pressure matters little; a rigid neck lowers head acceleration and HIC.

[36] Cecchi, N.J., Monroe, D.C., Moscoso, W., Hicks, J.W. and Reinkensmeyer, D.J. (2020) "Effects of soccer ball inflation pressure and velocity on peak linear and rotational accelerations of ball-to-head impacts", *Sports Engineering*, 23, article 16. ⚠️ *check co-author initials*
*Use for:* test head and neck at 34–76 kPa; lower pressure gave lower linear and rotational acceleration.

[37] England, R., Liverseidge, S., Miyazaki, Y., Oxford, S.W., Phillips, I. and Farmer, J. (2025) "Does reduced ball inflation pressure in association football decrease head impact kinematics?", *Annals of Biomedical Engineering*, 53(11). doi:10.1007/s10439-025-03804-0
*Use for:* cannon impacts at about 17 m/s, 0.6 vs 1.0 bar; lower pressure helped in most cases but is "not a universal solution".

[38] Austin, K., Smith, N. and Lauder, M. (2022) "The influence of football inflation pressure on impact force and head accelerations during football heading", *ISBS Proceedings Archive*, 40(1), article 12.
*Use for:* 12 players heading balls at 0.4 and 0.54 atm: lower impact force but no change in head acceleration.

[39] Becker, S., Berger, J., Backfisch, M., Ludwig, O., Kelm, J. and Fröhlich, M. (2019) "Effects of a 6-week strength training of the neck flexors and extensors on the head acceleration during headers in soccer", *Journal of Sports Science and Medicine*, 18(4), 729–737.
*Use for:* neck training gave no clear benefit (33 players).

[40] Peek, K., et al. (2022) "The effect of the FIFA 11+ with added neck exercises on maximal isometric neck strength and peak head impact magnitude during heading: a pilot study", *Sports Medicine*, 52, 655–668. ⚠️ *check the full author list*
*Use for:* neck exercises cut peak linear head acceleration by 11.8%, against 5.0% in controls (52 adolescents).
